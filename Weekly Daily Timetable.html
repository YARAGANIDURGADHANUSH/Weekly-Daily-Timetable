Why it is built this way
  • Hardest work first. Alertness for analytical thinking peaks in the first 2–5 hours after waking, so proofs and problem solving sit at 07:30–11:45. Hands-on coding follows the nap, and review and language work come in the evening.
  • Exercise before study. A morning run or walk raises attention for the next few hours. Run 3 days, walk and strength 2 days, one easy rest day. Add about 10% per week.
  • Nap logic. One sleep cycle is about 90 minutes. The 2-hour slot covers falling asleep, and the 15-minute buffer clears sleep inertia before you code. Night sleep of about 6.5 h plus the nap gives roughly 8 h.
  • Retrieval and spacing. The nightly review and Sunday review use recall and an error log. Recalling and redoing missed problems beats re-reading.
  • Interleaving. Discrete Math and Probability alternate by day, so you practise choosing the method rather than only repeating one. In Month 1 replace Probability with Discrete Math.
  • Short, daily language. Frequent short exposures beat one long weekly session.
  • Real rest. Breaks, free time and a screen-free wind-down protect memory consolidation. Without them the schedule fails by week 3.
  • Swap subjects with the dashboard. Blocks A/B follow the current month: Months 4–6 use Linear Algebra, Theory of Computation and Networks; Months 7–9 use Crypto, OS and Distributed Systems.
